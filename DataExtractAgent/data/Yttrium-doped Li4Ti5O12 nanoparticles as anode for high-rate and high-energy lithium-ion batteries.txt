Yttrium-doped   Li 4 Ti 5 O12 nanoparticles as anode for high-rate and high-energy lithium-ion batteries
Kai Su 1 · Chenxia Tang 1 · Chunyue Li 1 · Shijie Weng 1 · Yong Xiang 1,2,3,4 · Xiaoli Peng 1
Received: 14 October 2024 / Accepted: 17 December 2024
© The Author(s) 2024

Abstract
Li 4 Ti 5 O12  (LTO) batteries are known for safety and long lifespan due to zero-strain and stable lattice. However, their low specific capacity and lithium-ion diffusion limit practical use. This study explored modifying LTO through yttrium doping by hydrothermal method to form   Li 4 Y 0.2 Ti 4.8 O12  nanoparticles. This approach optimized electron and ion transport, markedly enhancing rate and cycle performance. XRD and TEM revealed that Y addition increased interplanar distance of LTO and widened   Li + transport pathways. XPS indicated that Y doping augmented the oxygen vacancy concentration and   Ti 3+ content. UV tests demonstrated a band gap reduction from 3.72 eV to 2.94 eV, accompanied by enhanced electronic conductivity. EIS tests showed lithium-ion diffusion coefficient remarkably increased to 1.27 ×   10 -10 cm 2 s -1 . The initial discharge capacity of   Li 4 Y 0.2 Ti 4.8 O12  at 1 A   g -1 reached 198.9 mAh   g -1 and retained 89.3% capacity after 1000 cycles. At 6 A   g -1 , the discharge capacity was 161.1 mAh   g -1 , while at an ultra-high current density of 20 A   g -1 , it reached 78.8 mAh   g -1 , highlighting its robust rate performance. The yttrium-doped and nano-morphology stabilizes the LTO lattice, enhancing rate performance and cycling stability. This study reveals that LTO has the potential to be used in the high-energy fast-charging storage market.
Keywords Li 4 Ti 5 O12  · Doping · Yttrium · High rate · Lithium-ion battery
1  Introduction
The advancement of energy storage plays a pivotal role in achieving carbon peaking and carbon neutrality goals [1, 2], necessitating energy storage systems characterized by high safety, extended operational life, and enhanced energy density [3, 4]. Presently, lithium-ion batteries dominate energy storage systems [5], with graphite and lithium titanate serving as primary materials on the anode side [6, 7].   Li 4 Ti 5 O12  (LTO), owing to its stable spinel crystal structure, exhibits negligible volume changes during the charge-discharge in the voltage of 1 to 2.5 V [8, 9]. LTO can inhibit lithium dendrite formation during cycling, positioning it as an excellent choice for high-safety anode applications [10, 11], particularly in

However, three electrons transfer in   [Li 3 ] 8a [LiTi 5 ] 16d [O 12 ] 32e results in a low discharge specific capacity (175 mAh   g -1 ) [12], alongside low   Li + diffusion coefficient   (10 -9 -10 -13 cm 2 s -1 ) [13]. These characteristics severely restrict the practical applications of lithium titanate as an anode material [14]. Consequently, there is a pressing research focus on enhancing the rate capability and discharge capacity of LTO within the scientific community.
Through methodologies such as surface coating [15, 16], structural design [17, 18], grain nanocrystallization [19, 20], two-phase composites [21, 22], ion doping [23-28] and other approaches, the electronic conductivity and rate capability of LTO can be enhanced. However, surface modification cannot typically improve the intrinsic lithium-ion diffusion coefficient of LTO, and intricate structural designs often involve complex fabrication processes [29]. In comparison, element doping can effectively promote the electronic conductivity and lithium-ion diffusion coefficient by either substituting the lattice positions or occupying the interstitial sites of LTO [30-32]. It has been demonstrated that doping   Ti 4+ with the rare earth element yttrium (Y) can effectively enhance the cycle stability and rate characteristics of LTO. For instance, Bai et al. [33] produced Y-doped LTO by co-precipitation method and achieved a capacity of 156.8 mAh   g -1 at 10C, about 90% capacity remained over 1800 cycles. Similarly, Chen et al. [34] prepared   Y 3+ doped LTO, surface-modified with   Y 2 Ti 2 O7 through solid-phase reaction, reached 92 mAh   g -1 after 1000 cycles at 40C. The heterogeneous valence doping of Y elements disrupts the charge balance, generating free electrons and oxygen vacancies that effectively alter the pathways for lithium-ion transport. Rare earth element Y is an excellent dopant, with the primary source area being South China. Y has a well-established and environmentally friendly recycling technology with the capacity for sustainable development [35, 36].
Reducing particle size can shorten the transport path of   Li + and enhance discharge capacity at high rates. Lu et al. [37] produced N-doped and carbon-coated LTO using a solvothermal method, composed of 3-5 nm nanoparticles and several nanometers thick nanosheets, showing a capacity of 208.5 mAh   g -1 at 1.6 A   g -1 . Zhu et al. [19] obtained Co-doped LTO nanosheets via a hydrothermal process, demonstrating a capacity of 106 mAh   g -1 (20C). Compared to the solidstate method, LTO synthesized by the solvothermal method results in a narrower particle size distribution and smaller particle size [10]. Nanoscale LTO possesses a larger specific surface area, thereby significantly increasing the discharge capacity at high currents. In addition, reducing the cut-off voltage can effectively enhance the capacity of LTO. However, additional lithium-ion insertion usually leads to severe polarization, contributing to drastic capacity degradation at high rates, affecting its cycling stability [38].
Therefore, it is imperative to devise a method that increases the active sites of LTO and stabilizes the lattice structure during high rate charge and discharge processes. In this study, Y-doped   Li 4 Y 0.2 Ti 4.8 O12  nanoparticles were prepared via the hydrothermal method. The nanostructure increased the specific surface area and active sites of   Li 4 Y 0.2 Ti 4.8 O12 , while its hollow structure facilitated electrolyte infiltration. Meanwhile, yttrium doping led to an increased interplanar distance of LTO and widened   Li + transport pathways. Y doping augmented the oxygen vacancy concentration and   Ti 3+ content in the lattice. Ultimately, the ion and electron transport properties were improved by multiple modifications through nanomorphology, and yttrium doping. This contributes to the improvement of the rate and cycle performance of the prepared Y-doped   Li 4 Ti 5 O12  nanoparticles. Even when discharged to a depth of 0.01 V, the initial discharge capacity of Li 4 Y 0.2 Ti 4.8 O12  reached 198.9 mAh   g -1 , and 89.3% of the capacity remained after 1000 cycles at 1 A   g -1 . At 10 A   g -1 , the discharge capacity is 131.3 mAh   g -1 . At a high current density of 20 A   g -1 , the specific capacity reached 78.8 mAh   g -1 . These results demonstrate markedly improved cyclic stability and high rate performance of   Li 4 Ti 5 O12 , underscoring its potential for widespread application in high energy density storage markets.
2    Experiment
2.1    Synthesis of nanoparticles   Li 4 Ti 5-x Y x O12
All raw materials were commercially purchased without further purification. Titanium tetrabutylate   (C 16 H36 O 4 Ti, Aladdin) served as the titanium source, yttrium nitrate hexahydrate   (N 3 O9 Y·6H 2 O, MERYER) as the source of yttrium, and lithium hydroxide monohydrate (LiOH·H 2 O, MERYER) as the lithium provider. Hexadecyl trimethyl ammonium bromide (CTAB, D&B) was utilized as the surfactant.
The synthesis procedure involved the preparation of two solutions: Solution A contained 0.88 g LiOH·H 2 O, 0.25 g CTAB, and an appropriate amount of   N 3 O9 Y·6H 2 O in 40 ml ethanol, heated and stirred for 30 min. Solution B comprised 10 ml

The stirred solution was transferred to a 100 ml polytetrafluoroethylene reactor and reacted at 200 ℃ for 16 h. The product was washed four times with ethanol and deionized water. The dried product underwent further treatment in a tube furnace at 600 ℃ for 5 h to prepare   Li 4 Y x T 5-x O12  powder. In the nomenclature, unmodified   Li 4 Ti 5 O12  is denoted as Y0. Samples with yttrium doping of X = 0.05, X = 0.1, X = 0.2 and X = 0.3 are designated as Y0.05, Y0.1, Y0.2 and Y0.3 respectively.
2.2    Structural characterization
The crystal structure was identified using X-ray diffraction (XRD, λ = 1.5406 Å, 10-90°, Rigaku Ultima IV, Japan) by powder at 2θ from 10° to 85° with a step speed of 2°   min -1 . The structural properties were further elucidated by Raman spectroscopy (Horiba LabRAM HR Evolution, Japan), which probed the optical vibration mode of the sample under ambient conditions. The morphology was observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Further characterization of morphology and elemental distribution was conducted using a transmission electron microscope (TEM, Japan Electronics F200), while crystal spacing was characterized using high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM). The chemical state of the samples was investigated by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo Scientific K-Alpha, USA), and the resulting data were subsequently analyzed using Avantage. To ascertain the presence of oxygen vacancies, electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) was conducted utilizing a Bruker EMXplus-6/1-EPR spectrometer. UV-visible diffuse reflectance tests were performed on Shimadzu UV-3600i Plus, Japan. The BET conditions (ASAP 2460 Micromeritics, US) were   N 2 , 77 K, and degassing temperature was 200 ℃ for 8 h. The pore size distribution was acquired using the BarrettJoyner-Halenda (BJH) method.
2.3    Electrochemical performance characterization
The anode was composed of the prepared active material powder, Super P and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) in a mass ratio of 7:2:1. Lithium metal as the counter electrode. The electrolyte was 1 M   LiPF 6 / EC: DMC: EMC (volume 1: 1: 1), and the Celgard 2500 membrane served as the separator. The CR2025 coin cell was assembled in the glove box (H 2 O,   O 2 < 0.1 ppm), and the assembled battery was left stationary for 24 h at room temperature before testing. At 25 ℃ , the charge/discharge tests were carried out on the LANDt battery test system with a voltage cut-off range of 0.01 to 3.0 V. Cycling tests were conducted at a charge/discharge current of 1 A   g -1 . Rate capability was assessed at 0.1 A   g -1 /0.2 A   g -1 /0.4 A   g -1 /1 A   g -1 /2 A   g -1 /4 A   g -1 /6 A   g -1 /10 A   g -1 /14 A   g -1 /20 A   g -1 /0.1 A   g -1 with the same charge/ discharge current density. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements (AMETEK-Princeton Versa STAT 3F) were performed in the frequency range of 0.01 Hz to 0.1 MHz. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was applied by using a CHI660E with scan rates ranging from 0.1 to 5 mV   s -1 , within a voltage window of 0.01 to 3.0 V.

Reducing particle size can enhance the rate performance of LTO, as nanoscale particles provide a larger specific surface area. This increase in surface area shortens the lithium-ion transport path, facilitates charge transfer, and accelerates electrode reactions [20]. Compared to solid state sintering, LTO prepared by the hydrothermal method exhibits smaller particle sizes. As shown in Fig. S1, the microstructure of the samples prepared via this method was characterized using SEM. The prepared samples, without further refinement, show a particle size distribution ranging from tens to hundreds of nanometers resulting from the aggregation of nanosized particles, and are characterized by a relatively uniform mesoporous distribution between particles. The micron-sized irregular clusters formed by the primary nanoparticles are beneficial as they increase the compression density of the anode, which in turn increases the battery's capacity density.
Nitrogen adsorption and desorption tests were conducted on samples with varying Y doping amounts, and the results are presented in Fig. 2a, with type IV adsorption curves for all the samples. Figure 2b demonstrates the BET specific surface area of Y0, Y0.05, Y0.1, Y0.2 and Y0.3, the specific surface areas were 49.87, 79.14, 98.40, 108.53 and 116.84   m 2 g -1 , respectively. The surface areas of the samples increased gradually with the elevation of the doping content, which is conducive to the penetration of the electrolyte during charge/discharge cycling [25]. The pore size of Y0 and Y0.2 are shown in Fig. 2c,d. It can be seen that the mesoporous range of Y0.2 sample is between 10 and 30 nm. The pore volume comparison graph is shown in Fig. 2e, where Y0.2 has the maximum hole capacity. Combined with SEM images (Fig. S1), there are a large number of mesoporous in the particles, which are likely to form a through-hole structure. This porous structure facilitates the transport of   Li + and allows electrolyte to fully penetrate into the electrode material, which is critical to improving rate performance [39].

As can be seen in Fig. 4a, Y doping can effectively improve rate capability when charging and discharging at high current density, which is due to the combined results of the means of doping with the heterovalent element Y and the nanoparticles with more active sites. It can be seen that the discharge capacity of Y0.2 decays slower with increasing current density, while Y0, Y0.05, Y0.1 and Y0.3 decay faster. At 4A   g -1 , Y0.2 has a capacity of 187.7 mAh   g -1 , whereas other components have discharge capacities less than 160 mAh   g -1 (Table S1). The discharge capacities of all modified samples exceeded 100 mAh   g -1 at 6 A   g -1 while Y0 has a capacity of 82.1 mAh   g -1 . Moreover, Y0.2 has a capacity of 131.3 mAh   g -1 at 10 A   g -1 , and even at 20 A   g -1 the discharge capacity still reached 78.8 mAh   g -1 . In contrast, Y0 exhibits a capacity of only 48.5 mAh   g -1 at 10 A   g -1 and has almost no capacity at 20 A   g -1 . When recovered to 0.1 A   g -1 , the capacity of Y0.2 is restored to 258.9 mAh   g -1 , indicating that Y doping improves the structural stability of the LTO when deeply discharged. From Fig. 4c, it can be seen that the discharge capacity of Y0.2 decays slower with increasing current density, while Y0 (Fig. 4b) decay faster. Table S1 lists the average discharge capacity of all samples at different current density.
In Fig. 4d, the rate performance of the Y0.2 clearly exceeds that of the other modifications reported in the literature listed (Table S2). The excellent performance of Y0.2 is attributed to the widening of lithium-ion transport channels due to the increased crystallite spacing by Y doping, as well as the nanosizing leads to huge specific surface area and more active sites. From the galvanostatic discharge-charge curves of Y0 (Fig. 4e) and Y0.2 (Fig. 4f) for different cycles, indicating that the Y0.2 has good electrochemical stability at high current density.
Figure 4g demonstrates the capacity curves of Y0, Y0.05, Y0.1, Y0.2 and Y0.3 for 1000 cycles at 1 A   g -1 . Pure LTO has the worst cycling stability with a capacity of only 41.2 mAh   g -1 after 1000 cycles, because of the large polarization under high current, and all modified samples performs better than Y0 with a capacity of more than 100 mAh   g -1 . After long cycling, Y0.05, Y0.1, Y0.2 and Y0.3 have a discharge capacity of 126.2 mAh   g -1 , 123.5 mAh   g -1 , 177.6 mAh   g -1 and 103.9 mAh   g -1 , respectively. Among them, Y0.2 presents the least capacity loss and the most stable cycling with a capacity retention of 89.3%.

In comparison to Y0, Y0.2 exhibits more distinct diffraction peaks, which suggests that Y0.2 possesses a more stable structure following the cycling process.

4    Conclusions
In general, Y-doped LTO nano-sized particles were prepared by a one-step hydrothermal method. XRD and TEM characterizations proved that Y doping increased the crystal plane spacing, and broadened the   Li + transport channels. The introduced Y increased grain boundaries in LTO, and the nano size particles promoted   Li + transportation and diffusion. XPS showed that the concentration of oxygen vacancies and   Ti 3+ had increased. Due to the co-modification of multiple methods, the band gap decreased and the lithium-ion diffusion coefficient increased significantly. Electrochemical tests showed that the Y0.2 can provide a discharge capacity of more than 250 mAh   g -1 at 0.1 A   g -1 , a capacity of 161.1 mAh g -1 at 6 A   g -1 , 131.3 mAh   g -1 at 10 A   g -1 , and even at an ultrahigh current density of 20 A   g -1 , the discharge capacity still reached 78.8 mAh   g -1 . After 1000 cycles at 1 A   g -1 , the discharge capacity was 177.6 mAh   g -1 , and the capacity retention was 89.3%. This study greatly improved the lattice stability and fast charge performance of LTO under deep discharge. It is expected that LTO can be applied to the energy storage market with high safety and high specific energy.
